NAZCA

The Nazca lines: giant figures — hummingbird, monkey, spider — drawn on a Peruvian desert plain in single unbroken strokes, each hundreds of meters long, laid down centuries before anyone could see them whole. A flat-cut toolpath is the same object: one continuous line on a plane that only makes sense once you understand the machine that walks it.

The bed. Flat-cut toolpaths, drawn like desert lines — one unbroken stroke.

IN ACTION

What it is

Nazca is the 2D bed inside Iris — the concept name for the flat-cut lane. Art dropped on the bed becomes one continuous toolpath: raster engraves, vector outlines, kerf-compensated cuts, for laser and plasma alike. Drag the art where it burns, size it in mm or inches, pick engrave or outline, and GENERATE draws the cut on the bed — amber for cuts, grey for travel, a red cross for every plasma pierce.

Nazca has no separate binary; the bed lives in the Iris prep window, and its manual is Iris's manual.
