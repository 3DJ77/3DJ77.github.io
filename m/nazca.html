NAZCA

STILL IN THE DEEP — surfacing when it clears the bench

The Nazca lines: giant figures — hummingbird, monkey, spider — drawn on a Peruvian desert plain in single unbroken strokes, each hundreds of meters long. A flat-cut toolpath is the same object: one continuous line on a plane that only makes sense once you understand the machine that walks it. Here the machine is a beam.

Laser & plasma prep — lines with meaning and matter.

Nazca is the flat-cut lane: everything that burns or pierces sheet stock. Raster engraving, vector outlines, SVG artwork and plasma cuts all land on the same bed in one top-down prep window. Bring in a drawing or a photo, get back a job the machine can run.

It is the module that turned a thirty-year-old pencil drawing into the sigils carved on the Obelisk: photograph, rectify, pull the linework, clean it, and hand it on to be embossed.

Nazca ships as a working test prototype with its rough edges written into the release notes.
